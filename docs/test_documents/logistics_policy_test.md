INTERNAL POLICY — Air Freight and Emergency Expediting

POLICY REFERENCE: POL-LOG-TEST-01
EFFECTIVE DATE: March 1, 2024
OWNER: Procurement and Logistics Director
REVIEW CYCLE: Annual

---

## PURPOSE

This policy defines the conditions under which air freight or emergency expediting
may be approved for inbound supply chain shipments. It is intended to ensure that
escalation decisions are consistent, cost-justified, and properly authorised.

---

## SCOPE

This policy applies to all procurement staff, supply chain planners, and operations
managers involved in managing inbound shipments and supplier delays.

---

## DEFINITIONS

- **Standard shipment**: sea or road freight within the agreed lead time
- **Expedited shipment**: air freight or courier used to reduce transit time
- **Emergency sourcing**: procurement from an alternate supplier outside the approved list
- **Delay threshold**: the number of days beyond original ETA that triggers escalation

---

## ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA FOR EXPEDITING

Air freight or expedited delivery may only be approved when ALL of the following
conditions are met:

1. The confirmed shipment delay is 5 or more days beyond the original ETA
2. The delayed part is on the critical path for one or more active production orders
3. Available stock will be exhausted before the revised ETA
4. The cost of expediting is less than the cost of production downtime

If condition 4 cannot be confirmed, the procurement manager must obtain written
approval from the Logistics Director before proceeding.

---

## APPROVAL THRESHOLDS

| Expediting Cost | Approval Required From |
|---|---|
| Below $5,000 | Procurement Manager |
| $5,000 to $20,000 | Logistics Director |
| Above $20,000 | VP of Operations |

All approvals must be recorded in the audit log with a rationale statement.
Verbal approvals are not accepted.

---

## COST RECOVERY

Air freight surcharges will be charged back to the Supplier if the delay was caused
by the Supplier's failure to meet the confirmed delivery date.
If the delay was caused by force majeure, the cost is absorbed by the Buyer.
Cost recovery claims must be submitted within 14 days of the expedited delivery.

---

## ALTERNATE SUPPLIER PROCEDURE

If expediting is not feasible or insufficient to prevent a production stoppage:

1. The supply chain planner raises an Emergency Sourcing Request
2. The procurement manager identifies approved alternate suppliers
3. Reliability score of the alternate supplier must be 0.80 or above
4. Lead time of the alternate supplier must be less than the remaining gap to stock-out
5. The Logistics Director approves the alternate supplier selection
6. A purchase order is raised and the original supplier is notified

The original supplier remains liable for the delay penalty as per the contract terms.

---

## ESCALATION PROCEDURE

Step 1 — Supply chain planner detects delay via system alert
Step 2 — Planner runs impact analysis to confirm shortage risk
Step 3 — Planner documents the situation and proposed action in the system
Step 4 — Procurement manager reviews and approves or escalates
Step 5 — Decision is recorded in the audit log with reviewer name and rationale
Step 6 — Outcome is reviewed at the monthly supplier performance meeting

---

## POLICY EXCEPTIONS

Exceptions to this policy require written approval from the VP of Operations.
All exceptions must be logged and reviewed at the next quarterly compliance review.
