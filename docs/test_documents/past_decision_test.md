PAST DECISION RECORD — Supplier Delay Recovery

DECISION ID: DEC-2023-047
DATE: August 14, 2023
REVIEWED BY: James Whitfield, Procurement Manager
EVENT: Supplier delay of 10 days on hydraulic valve shipment

---

## SITUATION SUMMARY

Supplier: Apex Components Ltd
Part affected: Hydraulic Control Valve HV-204 (equivalent to PRT-104)
Original ETA: August 10, 2023
Revised ETA: August 20, 2023
Delay: 10 days
Reason given by supplier: Port congestion at Shanghai due to typhoon advisory

Available stock at detection: 180 units
Daily consumption rate: 15 units per day
Stock coverage: 12 days (stock runs out approximately August 22)
Shortage risk: YES — revised ETA (Aug 20) is 2 days before stock-out (Aug 22)
Financial exposure: $34,500 (two production orders at risk)

---

## OPTIONS EVALUATED

Option A — Use existing stock:
  Feasibility: FEASIBLE
  Cost: $0 additional
  Note: Stock runs out August 22, shipment arrives August 20. Stock just covers demand.
  Score: 95.0 / 100

Option B — Transfer from secondary warehouse WH-03:
  Feasibility: FEASIBLE
  Available units at WH-03: 60 units
  Transfer time: 2 days
  Cost: $1,200 (freight)
  Score: 82.0 / 100

Option C — Expedite shipment via air freight:
  Feasibility: INFEASIBLE
  Reason: Supplier confirmed goods are not yet ready at origin port.
  Air freight cannot reduce the manufacturing delay.

Option D — Alternate supplier (FastParts Inc):
  Feasibility: FEASIBLE
  Reliability score: 0.88
  Lead time: 8 days (arrival approximately August 22)
  Cost: $18,500 (premium pricing plus air freight)
  Score: 61.0 / 100
  Note: Arrives same day as stock-out. Tight but acceptable.

---

## DECISION MADE

APPROVED: Option A — Use existing stock
Rationale: Stock covers demand through to the revised ETA with a 2-day buffer.
No additional cost required. The port delay qualifies as force majeure so no
penalty clause was invoked against the supplier.

The situation was monitored daily. The shipment arrived August 19 (one day early).
No production orders were disrupted. No customer deliveries were missed.

---

## LESSONS LEARNED

1. Port congestion events require same-day assessment of stock coverage.
   In this case the 2-day buffer was tight. A 1-day further delay would have
   required activating Option B immediately.

2. The alternate supplier (FastParts Inc) should be formally added to the
   approved supplier list for emergency sourcing. Their quote turnaround was
   4 hours, which is acceptable for emergency response.

3. The force majeure documentation from the supplier was received within 24 hours,
   which is within the policy requirement. This process worked correctly.
